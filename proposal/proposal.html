DentiFlow: Shark-Skin-Inspired Microtextured Luminal Surfaces for EVAR Stent Grafts

Project Proposal  |  Sanhita Tummala

1. Problem and Rationale

The clinical problem. Endovascular aneurysm repair (EVAR) is now the most common way to treat abdominal aortic aneurysms. A fabric-covered stent graft lines the aneurysm, so blood flows over a large polymer surface (woven polyester or ePTFE). That surface fails in two ways:

  • Thrombosis: limb graft occlusion affects 0.7–12.4% of patients [1].
  • Infection: rare, at 0.6% of patients, but pooled 30-day mortality is 26.6% [2].

The surface matters. Polyester grafts are more thrombogenic than ePTFE in whole blood under flow, which matches their clinical occlusion rates [3]. Limb occlusion is linked to low time-averaged wall shear stress (TAWSS), high oscillatory shear index (OSI) and high relative residence time (RRT) [4]. The resting infrarenal aorta already has low, oscillatory shear [5], below the 0.4 Pa level associated with thrombosis [6].

Why shark skin. Shark-skin denticles (100–200 µm scales with riblets) reduce drag in turbulent flow [7], and a 2 µm Sharklet™ micropattern reduces Staphylococcus aureus biofilm [8]. Staphylococci are the leading endograft pathogens. Graft flow, however, is laminar and oscillatory. Denticle-scale grooves sit deep inside its ≈ 1 mm boundary layer, where deep grooves can trap stagnant eddies [9]. So drag reduction cannot simply be assumed to transfer; this project tests it.

Research question: Can a denticle-inspired texture on the luminal surface of an EVAR graft reduce platelet adhesion and bacterial biofilm compared with a smooth graft, without worsening the low-shear, oscillatory near-wall flow?
Original hypothesis (tested computationally, §2): a height-neutral (recessed) denticle texture with low groove aspect ratio keeps TAWSS, OSI and RRT within 5% of a smooth surface while reducing simulated platelet deposition. Result: not supported. No texture met the 5% TAWSS criterion.

2. Computational Study (completed)

Method. This is a two-scale OpenFOAM model. An earlier coronary pilot used the same Carreau blood and Lagrangian Sorensen platelet kinetics [10].

  1. Vessel scale. An idealized bifurcated endograft (25 mm body, 12 mm legs, 16 mm limbs) with pulsatile resting infrarenal inflow (0.9 L/min) and a Womersley inlet. This step finds the worst low-shear site.
  2. Texture scale. A periodic wall patch, one texture unit cell, driven by that site's shear waveform. It is solved at 6 shear levels and the cardiac cycle is rebuilt from them.
  3. Platelets. Platelets deposit on a reactive graft surface. Each design gets 3 seeds of 20 s, and the metric is the deposition rate over 10–20 s.

Checks: straight tube vs exact Womersley solution, 1.05% RMS error (TAWSS 0.191 vs 0.187 Pa); graft cycle-to-cycle change < 0.15%; patch mesh 3 vs 2 µm, WSS +4% (the coarser mesh flatters textures, so penalties are conservative); quasi-steady assumption vs exact unsteady solve, TAWSS within 0.1%.

Vessel result. TAWSS is 0.23 Pa in the body and 0.33 Pa in the limbs; 94% of the limb area is below 0.4 Pa. The worst site is the outer limb wall just past the flare: TAWSS 0.35 Pa, OSI 0.47, RRT 103 Pa−1, with almost no net forward shear.

Findings.

  1. Every texture lowered wall shear on the graft. The penalty grows with groove depth and with added wetted area. Turning the features across the flow was worst. Riblets aligned with the flow and shallow denticles cost least. OSI changed by under 4%, and reversing the crowns changed nothing, as expected for creeping (Stokes) flow at this scale.
  2. Height-neutral placement did not remove the penalty. It traded protrusion for slow fluid trapped in the field between crowns.
  3. Simulated platelet benefits were small, at 3–8%. They arise because slower groove flow carries fewer platelets to the wall. The model omits agonist build-up in that same stagnant fluid, which is how low shear promotes clotting in vivo. So the computational case for hydrodynamic-scale denticles is weak.

Revised hypothesis (bench): under flow at the graft's wall shear (≈ 0.35 Pa), hydrodynamic-scale textures (15–43 µm) will not reduce platelet adhesion by more than about 10% compared with smooth. Any meaningful anti-adhesion and anti-biofilm benefit will come from cell-scale (2 µm) micropatterns, which add almost no wall-shear penalty.

Dependent variables. CFD: TAWSS, OSI, RRT, slow-flow volume, platelet deposition rate. Bench: adhered platelets per mm² (fluorescence count or LDH assay) and S. aureus biofilm (crystal violet, OD590). Constants: polymer, coupon size, PRP donor pool per day, shear stress, exposure time.

Bench surfaces (7): (1) smooth; (2) PET-like ridges; (3) shallow riblets; (4) 20 µm and (5) 43 µm height-neutral denticles, the latter testing the model's prediction; (6) a Sharklet-type 2 µm pattern [8]; (7) hierarchical, 20 µm denticles with the 2 µm pattern on top.

4. Bench Methods and Timeline

  1. Fabrication (months 1–2). Make molds by DLP/SLA printing (for the 15–43 µm features) and by photolithography or purchased masters (for the 2 µm pattern). Cast PDMS negatives and imprint the texture into polyurethane or PLGA films. Check transfer by profilometry or SEM, and accept a coupon when the measured feature height is within ±20% of design.
  2. Platelets under flow (months 2–4). Run a parallel-plate flow chamber at 0.35 Pa, with oscillation if the pump allows, because the CFD shows the effect depends on flow. Run static PRP adhesion as a secondary test.
  3. Biofilm (months 3–5). Grow S. aureus biofilm (BSL-2, or a BSL-1 surrogate) for 24 h, static.
  4. Statistics. Use n ≥ 3 coupons per surface on 3 separate days. Analyse with a one-way ANOVA and Dunnett's test against smooth (α = 0.05). Report effect sizes with 95% confidence intervals.
  5. Pending CFD. Replace the generic waveform with a digitised MRI waveform [5], add an exercise flow case, and rerun the screen, including the 2 µm pattern.

Limitations. The graft model is idealized and rigid. The waveform shape is generic. The platelet model is simplified: no red cells, no agonist fields, no detachment, and rate constants from collagen. Because deposition is limited by diffusion, the result for protruding designs depends on the modelling choice of where the well-mixed blood begins. Coupons test the surface, not the whole device.

Expected outcome. The computational result is already a useful negative: shark-skin drag-reduction geometry does not transfer to the slow, oscillating flow in a graft limb, and recessing it does not fix that. The bench study tests whether a texture can still help, at the cell scale, where platelets and bacteria actually attach.
