DentiFlow: Shark-Skin-Inspired Microtextured Luminal Surfaces for EVAR Stent Grafts

Project Proposal  |  Sanhita Tummala

1. Problem and Rationale

The clinical problem. Endovascular aneurysm repair (EVAR) is now the most common way to treat abdominal aortic aneurysms. A fabric-covered stent graft is placed inside the aneurysm, and blood then flows over a large polymer surface (woven polyester or ePTFE) instead of the native vessel wall. That surface fails in two ways: thrombosis (clot forms inside the graft, and limb graft occlusion affects 0.7–12.4% of patients [1]) and infection (rare at 0.6%, but deadly, with pooled 30-day mortality of 26.6% [2]).

Evidence that the surface matters. In a dynamic whole-blood test, the polyester grafts were markedly more thrombogenic than ePTFE, in line with the devices' clinical occlusion rates [3]. CFD studies tie limb occlusion to low time-averaged wall shear stress (TAWSS) and high oscillatory shear index (OSI) and relative residence time (RRT) [4]. The resting infrarenal aorta already has low, oscillatory shear [5]. A 12 mm graft limb carrying about 0.5 L/min has a Poiseuille mean WSS of only ≈ 0.2–0.3 Pa, below the 0.4 Pa level linked to thrombosis and atherogenesis [6]. So the graft's luminal surface sits in a slow, oscillating flow that favours clotting, and it can be engineered.

Why shark skin, and the gap being tested. Shark skin is covered in dermal denticles: scales about 100–200 µm long whose crowns carry riblets. They reduce drag in turbulent flow [7]. A related micropattern (Sharklet™, 2 µm features) reduces Staphylococcus aureus biofilm [8]. Staphylococci are the leading endograft pathogens. But drag reduction does not carry over directly. Infrarenal flow is mostly laminar (peak Re ≈ 2,000; Womersley number ≈ 10). Riblets work at about 15 viscous wall units, which at aortic shear means riblets ≈ 1 mm apart, about 10× larger than a denticle's. Denticle-scale features sit deep inside the ≈ 1 mm oscillatory boundary layer, where deep grooves can trap stagnant eddies [9].

The project therefore tests the mechanisms that could transfer (groove flushing, contact area, cell-scale anti-adhesion) rather than assuming drag reduction.

Research question: Can a denticle-inspired texture on the luminal surface of an EVAR graft reduce platelet adhesion and bacterial biofilm formation, compared with a smooth graft surface, without worsening the low-shear, oscillatory near-wall flow?
Hypothesis: A height-neutral (recessed) denticle texture with low groove aspect ratio will reduce simulated and measured platelet adhesion and staphylococcal biofilm compared with a smooth surface, while keeping TAWSS, OSI and RRT within 5% of the smooth surface.

2. Preliminary Work (coronary pilot, completed)

I built and validated an OpenFOAM CAD-to-CFD pipeline (Poiseuille WSS error −2.5%). It uses Carreau non-Newtonian blood, pulsatile inflow and Lagrangian platelet deposition with Sorensen kinetics [10]. I ran it on a 3.2 mm coronary stent with 15 designs. Two findings carry over directly to EVAR:

  1. Protrusion costs hemodynamics. Denticles placed on top of struts raised the low-WSS wall area from 23.5% to 25–30%, in proportion to their height. This motivates recessed textures.
  2. Shape dominates platelet adhesion. Two designs of equal height (≈ 42 µm) gave −24% and +91% adhesion against the same control. Undercut crowns with steep upstream faces were the worst.

I moved to EVAR because covered coronary stents are a niche bailout device and coronary infection is negligible. EVAR is where covered surfaces are standard and both thrombosis and infection matter.

Objectives (dependent variables). CFD: maximize patch TAWSS; minimize low-WSS area (< 0.4 Pa), OSI, RRT, stagnant groove volume and simulated platelet deposition per mm². Bench: platelet adhesion (LDH assay or fluorescence counts) and biofilm (crystal violet, OD590).

Controls: smooth surface (ePTFE-like) and a woven/crimped polyester-like texture (clinical comparator). Constants: graft geometry, infrarenal waveform, Carreau blood properties, patch size, mesh settings. Strategy: (1) one-factor-at-a-time screen; (2) Stat-Ease central-composite response surface over net height, h/s and crown shape; (3) rank on a composite score and report the Pareto front between hemodynamics and platelet deposition.

4. Design Inspirations and Candidate Designs

  • Mako / blacktip denticles [7]: overlapping crowns with 3–5 riblets. These are the basis for the parametric family and for my existing CAD crown.
  • Recessed (engraved) denticles (new): the crown embossed into the graft coating, so the surface adds no protrusion. This is the key test of the hypothesis.
  • Continuous vs segmented riblets: streamwise grooves compared with discrete scales, to test groove flushing.
  • Native polyester crimp: an "accidental" transverse texture already on commercial grafts, and the clinical baseline to beat.
  • Sharklet-type micropattern [8]: 2 µm ribs in diamond arrays, an anti-staphylococcal design. It will be tested alone and layered onto denticles (hierarchical).

5. Methods Overview and Timeline

  1. Macro-scale CFD (months 1–2): idealized bifurcated endograft (24 mm body, 12 mm limbs, rigid walls) with resting and exercise infrarenal waveforms [5] and Carreau blood; validate against the analytical Womersley solution; map TAWSS, OSI and RRT and pick the worst low-shear site in the limbs.
  2. Wall-patch CFD (months 2–3): a 2 × 1 mm textured patch with periodic sides, driven by the near-wall shear waveform from step 1; mesh-independence study (accept when metrics change < 5%); run the DOE with the platelet model on a reactive graft surface, 3 seeds per design.
  3. Fabrication (months 3–4): DLP/SLA-printed or PDMS soft-lithography negative molds; imprint into PLGA films, plus flat and polyester-texture controls; verify texture transfer by microscopy and profilometry.
  4. Biological testing (months 4–6), at a supervised institutional lab with SRC/IRB pre-approval: platelet-rich plasma adhesion (n ≥ 3 coupons per surface, 3 separate days); staphylococcal biofilm (BSL-2 S. aureus, or a BSL-1 surrogate per fair rules); one-way ANOVA with Dunnett's test vs the smooth control (α = 0.05). Optional: parallel-plate flow chamber at the CFD-predicted shear (0.2–1 Pa).

Limitations. Idealized, rigid-walled graft geometry. The patch model assumes the texture does not change vessel-scale flow (valid because features are under 1% of the limb diameter). Simplified platelet model. PLGA degrades over weeks to months, so a permanent polymer such as polyurethane would be needed clinically. Coupons test surface effects, not whole-device performance.

Expected outcome. Either result is useful. A positive result identifies a drug-free texture for graft limbs, where clotting risk is highest. A negative result shows quantitatively why shark-skin benefits do not transfer to laminar, low-shear blood flow.
